# Lesson: highland cow

The step pictures are in steps/, and each step beside the one before it in pairs/.

## Step 1: Blend the sunset sky
Part of: The sky
Picture: steps/01.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/01.jpg)
Paints:
- Sky blue (#87a1c2): 2 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow, a touch of burnt sienna
- Cream gold (#d5b48b): 2 parts white, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow
- Pale gold (#f6bc63): 3 parts white, 2 parts yellow, 1 part red, 1 part burnt sienna
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. Inside the outlined area. It reaches all four edges of the canvas.
2. Paints, top to bottom: Sky blue, Cream gold, then Pale gold.
3. Mix enough Cream gold for this step and steps 6 and 20; cover it between them.
4. Mix enough Pale gold for this step and steps 3 and 19; cover it between them.
5. Brush: 1 inch flat. Load: Creamy paint. An even coat.
6. Start with the darkest color and stroke side to side. Without rinsing, pick up the next lighter color and paint below it. Then stroke back up over the join several times.
7. It should leave soft side-to-side bands of blue and gold over the whole canvas, with a soft join where two wet colors meet.
8. Watch out: Blending after the paint has started to drag. Acrylic sets in minutes, and scrubbing setting paint lifts it and leaves streaks. Fix: stop as soon as it drags.
9. Let it dry, then blend a new thin layer on top.
10. Step back, squint, or take a photo to see it whole, and compare the big shapes with the picture.
Tip: The light comes from a low setting sun at the right edge of the canvas, so keep the palest gold on the right side of the sky, where the after picture has it, and let the blue show more on the left side. Cover the whole canvas, about 12 in wide and 16 in tall, not just the top: the bands of Sky blue, Cream gold and Pale gold repeat all the way to the bottom edge, and the hills and cow will be painted over them later. Keep every stroke running side to side with a slight wave, like long thin clouds, rather than straight stripes.

## Step 2: Dry-brush the cloud streaks
Part of: The sunset clouds
Picture: steps/02.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/02.jpg)
Paints:
- Warm peach (#eaae71): 3 parts white, 1 part yellow, 1 part red, 1 part burnt sienna
- Dusky rose (#c1ae9c): 12 parts white, 3 parts red, 3 parts ultramarine, 2 parts yellow
Brush: 1/2 inch flat
Words:
1. Let the sky dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area, all the way across from the top edge. Its bottom is about 2 in from the top edge.
3. The outline also runs under where the green hills and meadow, and the far blue mountains will go: paint straight through there.
4. The outline also reaches onto the sunset sky, painted already: there, only soften this paint into its edge and leave it showing.
5. Paints, darkest first: Dusky rose, then Warm peach. The paint map shows where each goes.
6. Mix enough Warm peach for this step and step 5; cover it between them.
7. Brush: 1/2 inch flat. Load: Wiped nearly dry. Only the tip goes in the paint, and no water on the brush. It should feel dry and hard to apply.
8. Light pressure, skimming the surface.
9. It should leave feathery, broken, see-through marks that catch the canvas texture. Never a solid area.
10. Watch out: Hard, solid clouds. Too much paint on the brush. Fix: wipe the brush nearly dry and skim long sideways strokes with the tip, so the sky shows through the edges.
Tip: Run every stroke sideways, the long way across the sunset sky, so the streaks lie in thin flat bands like the after picture's. Lay the Dusky rose first in a few short streaks, then drag Warm peach along and between them, leaving the blue of the sunset sky showing through their broken edges.

## Step 9: Dab the dark bushes on the hills

## Step 23: Line the nostrils and mouth
Part of: The face
Picture: steps/23.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/23.jpg)
Paints:
- Dark umber (#7b4228): 3 parts red, 2 parts ultramarine, 2 parts burnt sienna, a touch of white
Brush: #2 liner
Words:
1. Let the sunlit rim and the shaggy coat dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its bottom is about 6 in from the bottom edge, and its right side is about 4 in from the right edge; it's about 2 in wide and 2 1/2 in tall.
3. The outline also reaches onto the cow's shadows and the highland cow, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
4. Paint: Dark umber.
5. Brush: #2 liner. Load: Thin paint. Swirl the brush in water, then in the paint. Add water if the line skips. Reload often.
6. Hold the brush upright, with light, steady pressure. For a tapering line, start thick and lighten as you go. Let lines fork in different directions and overlap, and wobble the edges a little.
7. It should leave thin, flowing lines.
8. Leave what steps 21 and 22 painted showing around these marks, as in the Result picture.
9. Watch out: Skippy, broken lines. The paint is too thick. Fix: add water.
10. Step back, squint, or take a photo to see it whole, and compare the big shapes with the picture.
Tip: Paint the two nostrils first, as small commas at the bottom of the nose where the after picture has them, then the mouth line under the nose. Keep both thin so the Nose pink top and the Rosy nose stay showing. The after picture also turns the whole nose a slightly greyer pink with a darker shadow under the mouth; if yours looks too bright, a thin touch of Dark umber softened under the mouth gets you close.
