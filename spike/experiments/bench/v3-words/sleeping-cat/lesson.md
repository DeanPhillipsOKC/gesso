# Lesson: sleeping cat

The step pictures are in steps/, and each step beside the one before it in pairs/.

## Step 1: Blend the sky in the window
Part of: The view through the window
Picture: steps/01.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/01.jpg)
Paints:
- Pale sky blue (#a7d8f9): 3 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow
- Warm white (#fbf1da): 12 parts white, 1 part yellow, a touch of red
Brush: 3/4 inch wash brush
Words:
1. Inside the outlined area. Its top is at the top edge, and its left side is at the left edge; it's about 1 1/4 in wide and 1 3/4 in tall.
2. Paints, top to bottom: Pale sky blue, then Warm white.
3. Mix enough Warm white for this step and steps 5, 15 and 21; cover it between them.
4. Brush: 3/4 inch wash brush. Load: Creamy paint. An even coat.
5. Start with the darkest color and stroke side to side. Without rinsing, pick up the next lighter color and paint below it. Then stroke back up over the join several times.
6. It should leave smooth bands of color, with a soft join where two wet colors meet.
7. Watch out: Blending after the paint has started to drag. Acrylic sets in minutes, and scrubbing setting paint lifts it and leaves streaks. Fix: stop as soon as it drags.
8. Let it dry, then blend a new thin layer on top.
Tip: The light comes through this window from the upper left, so the sky is the brightest, coolest color here: keep it pale. The sky through the window reaches the left and top edges of the canvas; leave its ragged lower edge loose, as the after picture shows, because the leaves and the white window frame cover it later.

## Step 2: Dab the dark leaves
Part of: The view through the window
Picture: steps/02.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/02.jpg)
Paints:
- Leaf green (#83b5a2): 3 parts white, 2 parts ultramarine, 1 part yellow
Brush: old stiff brush
Words:
1. Let the sky through the window dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its top is about 1/2 in from the top edge, and its left side is about 1 in from the left edge; it's about 1/4 in wide and 1 1/4 in tall.
3. The outline also runs under where the white window frame and the frame's shadows will go: paint straight through there.
4. The outline also reaches onto the sky through the window, painted already: there, only soften this paint into its edge and leave it showing.
5. Paint: Leaf green.
6. Brush: old stiff brush. Load: A light load. No water on the brush: a wet one makes blobs.
7. Tap with the tip of the brush. The more you tap one spot, the more solid it gets.
8. It should leave dots or dappled patches that get more solid the more you tap.
9. Watch out: Blobs instead of dots. The brush is too wet or holds too much paint. Fix: wipe most of the paint off on a paper towel and tap again.
Tip: Tap the clumps in loose groups and leave small gaps of the pale sky through the window between them, as the after picture shows. Make the darkest, most solid tapping along the lower part of the clumps, away from the light at the upper left. The after picture shows these marks as a deeper blue-green over the sky rather than a clear green; match its darker patches and where it leaves the pale sky.

## Step 4: Blend the blue wall
Part of: The wall and the window frame

- Wall blue (#6d96c7): 1 part ultramarine, 1 part white, a touch of yellow

## Step 8: Shade the sill's front edge

## Step 19: Paint the pink nose
Part of: The sleeping face
Picture: steps/19.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/19.jpg)
Paints:
- Soft pink (#c58f84): 6 parts white, 3 parts burnt sienna, 1 part red, 1 part ultramarine
Brush: #4 round
Words:
1. Let the fur, and the stripes and ears dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its top is about 1 3/4 in from the top edge, and its left side is about 5 in from the left edge; it's about 1 1/4 in wide and 5 in tall.
3. The outline also reaches onto the sleeping tabby cat, and the cat's stripes and pink ears, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
4. Paint: Soft pink.
5. Brush: #4 round. Load: Creamy paint, a full load.
6. Touch down with the tip, press so the belly spreads into a small rounded triangle, then pull down and lift so it ends on the tip.
7. It should leave a small rounded triangle, wide at the top and pointed at the bottom.
8. Watch out: A big blob instead of a small nose. Too much paint, or too slow a lift. Fix: use less paint and lift faster.
Tip: The nose is tiny, a rounded triangle pointing down, sitting where the white muzzle meets the gray face, as the after picture shows. Make one press-and-lift and stop; a bigger nose makes the cat look awake. Leave the white of the muzzle showing all around it.

## Step 20: Line the closed eyes and mouth
Part of: The sleeping face
Picture: steps/20.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/20.jpg)
Paints:
- Charcoal (#695e5e): 2 parts ultramarine, 2 parts burnt sienna, 1 part white, a touch of yellow
Brush: #1 liner
Words:
1. Let the fur, and the stripes and ears dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its top is about 1 1/2 in from the top edge, and its left side is about 1 in from the left edge; it's about 6 in wide and 5 1/2 in tall.
3. The outline also reaches onto the sleeping tabby cat, and the cat's stripes and pink ears, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
4. Paint: Charcoal.
5. Brush: #1 liner. Load: Thin paint. Swirl the brush in water, then in the paint. Add water if the line skips. Reload often.
6. Hold the brush upright, with light, steady pressure. Pull each eye as one smooth curve, starting thick and lightening toward its outer end so it tapers.
7. It should leave thin, curved lines.
8. Leave what step 19 painted showing around these marks, as in the Result picture.
9. Watch out: Skippy, broken lines. The paint is too thick. Fix: add water.
Tip: Each closed eye is one smooth curve that bows downward, like a smile turned over: the eye nearer the window is the longer, flatter curve, and the other sits higher and slants, as the after picture shows. Paint the eyes first, then the tiny mouth line just under the nose. The after picture also shows a few faint white whisker lines beside the face already; leave them for step 21.

## Step 21: Pull the whiskers
Part of: The sleeping face
Picture: steps/21.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/21.jpg)
Paints:
- Warm white (#fbf1da): 12 parts white, 1 part yellow, a touch of red
Brush: #1 liner
Words:
1. Let the fur, and the stripes and ears dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Rinse the brush well first. Dirty water tints every color with the last one.
3. Inside the outlined area. Its bottom is about 5 1/2 in from the bottom edge, and its left side is about 5 1/2 in from the left edge; it's about 1/2 in wide and 1/4 in tall.
4. The outline also reaches onto the cat's shading and white patches, and the sleeping tabby cat, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
5. Paint: Warm white.
6. Brush: #1 liner. Load: Thin paint. Swirl the brush in water, then in the paint. Add water if the line skips. Reload often.
7. Hold the brush upright, with light, steady pressure. Start each whisker at the muzzle and pull outward, starting thick and lightening so it tapers to a fine point. Let them fan apart and cross a little.
8. It should leave thin, tapering lines.
9. Leave what steps 19 and 20 painted showing around these marks, as in the Result picture.
10. Watch out: Skippy, broken lines. The paint is too thick. Fix: add water.
11. Step back, squint, or take a photo to see it whole, and compare the big shapes with the picture.
Tip: Start each whisker at the muzzle and pull outward in a gentle curve, three or four on each side of the muzzle, fanning apart as they go, as the after picture shows. The longer ones sweep out across the gray cheek toward the white chest patch; the shorter ones sweep out over the top of the white paws. Keep them thin enough that the gray fur shows between them.
