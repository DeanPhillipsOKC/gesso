# Lesson: birch grove

The step pictures are in steps/, and each step beside the one before it in pairs/.

## Before you start

You're going to paint a misty autumn birch grove, with a leaf-covered path running back into the fog. The heart of it is the pair of big leaning birches against the gold haze, with the path opening next to them. What makes it sing is the contrast: warm gold leaves and white bark in front of cool blue fog.

The light: The low sun shines through the trees from behind, so the near side of the wood glows gold while the pines and the far side sit in cool blue shade. The birch trunks and the leaves catch that sun on the side facing it.

What this painting teaches:
- Dabbing (steps 6, 7, 8, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22): Dabbing is tapping with the tip of a nearly dry brush, so each tap leaves a leaf. It builds the golden bushes, the leaves overhead and the carpet of fallen leaves, always dark first, then gold, then the sunlit ones. Watch for blobs: they mean a wet or overloaded brush, so wipe it on a towel.
- Dry brush (steps 3, 10, 15, 17): Dry brush is a brush with almost no paint and no water, skimmed lightly so the paint catches only the canvas texture. It makes the mist, the shadow on the path, and the shade and light on the birch bark. If it goes on solid, the brush held too much paint.
- Thick to thin (steps 2, 14, 16): Thick to thin is pressing the full width of the brush, then turning onto its edge and easing off so the mark tapers. It pulls the far misty trunks, the big birch trunks and the dark bark marks. Press firmly where the mark should be wide, and lift gently where it should end.

The way there: First comes the misty background: the blue glow, a far trunk and a veil of mist, so the depth is there before anything solid goes on. Next the near woods: the dark pines and the golden bushes, each built dark to light. Then the ground and its path, with a shadow and the mossy rock. The birches come after that, over everything behind them, because they are the subject. Last come the gold leaves overhead, the fallen leaves, and the brightest sunlit leaves that finish it.

This stage, The misty background: This stage lays the foggy blue wood that everything else stands in.

## Step 1: Blend the misty glow
Part of: The misty background
Picture: steps/01.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/01.jpg)
Paints:
- Birch gray-white (#d9d1c6): 6 parts white, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow, a touch of ultramarine
- Golden yellow (#e78f1a): 2 parts yellow, 1 part red, a touch of ultramarine
- Haze blue (#9bb2ce): 2 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. Start: Start across the top edge with Haze blue and work down. This coat covers the whole canvas, from edge to edge.
2. Strokes: Work in short criss-cross X strokes, with both colors on the brush where two meet, so they melt together. Keep going down to the bottom edge.
3. Where the paint sits: Most of it is Haze blue, darker in soft upright patches up high, like trees in fog. One thin warm band of Golden yellow runs in from the left edge, fading before the middle: the low sun.
4. Leave: No bare canvas anywhere. The criss-cross marks can stay showing as soft texture.
5. Stop when: A cool blue, foggy wood, lightened low down with Birch gray-white into pale mottled fog. Don't blend it flat; stop while it looks soft and broken.

This stage, The misty background: This stage lays the foggy blue wood that everything else stands in.

## Step 2: Pull the far misty trunks
Part of: The misty background
Picture: steps/02.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/02.jpg)
Paints:
- Misty blue-gray (#a1afc0): 3 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow, a touch of burnt sienna
Brush: 1/2 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the glow from step 1 dry completely, or these trunks will smear into it.
2. Start: Start with the one long trunk, its left side about 3 1/2 in from the left edge.
3. Strokes: Press the full brush width at its foot, then pull straight up, turning onto the edge and easing off so it thins. It is about 5 3/4 in tall.
4. Where the paint sits: Add two short, soft marks the same way: a small one nearer the left edge, and a narrow one near the top edge, about 11 in from the left edge. Far trunks get no bark marks and no light.
5. Leave: Leave the rest of the glow untouched.
6. Stop when: One thin blue-gray trunk tapering upward and two small soft marks, all a little darker than the fog. If a trunk looks crisp, it looks too close: keep them soft.

This stage, The misty background: This stage lays the foggy blue wood that everything else stands in. By its end: The whole canvas is a soft blue fog with faint pale trunks, darker tree shapes and a faint warm glow.

## Step 3: Dry-brush the mist down the path
Part of: The misty background
Picture: steps/03.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/03.jpg)
Paints:
- Mist white (#d6e0ee): 12 parts white, 1 part ultramarine
Brush: 1 inch old stiff flat
Words:
1. First: Steps 1 and 2 must be dry, or the dry brush will drag them.
2. Start: Start where the far trunks fade into the fog in the middle of the canvas, with only the bristle tips in the paint.
3. Strokes: Skim the brush lightly side to side, barely touching, so the paint catches only the texture of the canvas. Build it in a few light passes.
4. Where the paint sits: Keep it to a faint veil over the lower ends of the far trunks, so they fade into the fog where the path will end.
5. Leave: Everything else stays as it is, and the trunks still show through the veil.
6. Stop when: The change is very slight: the fog there just looks a little softer and paler. If a solid white patch appears, the brush held too much paint: wipe it and skim more lightly.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage builds the dark pines and the golden bushes that make the near woods.

## Step 4: Stamp the dark pines
Part of: The pines and the golden bushes
Picture: steps/04.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/04.jpg)
Paints:
- Pine blue-green (#586e80): 1 part ultramarine, 1 part white, a touch of burnt sienna
Brush: fan brush
Words:
1. First: The fog must be dry before you stamp over it.
2. Start: Start at the pine tops near the top edge. The pine mass's left side is about 9 in from the left edge.
3. Strokes: Stamp with the middle tips of the fan, working down each tree in a zigzag and pressing harder as you go, so each tree widens toward its foot.
4. Where the paint sits: Make several pointed tree shapes side by side, merging into one dark mass low down. No light yet: this side of the wood is in shade.
5. Leave: Leave small gaps of fog between branches and trees. Everything left of the blue-green pines on the right stays as it is.
6. Stop when: A flat, dark mass of pines from the top edge down past the middle of the canvas, fog peeking through. Clumpy marks? Thin the paint and spread the fan.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage builds the dark pines and the golden bushes that make the near woods.

## Step 5: Tap light on the pines' left side
Part of: The pines and the golden bushes
Picture: steps/05.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/05.jpg)
Paints:
- Sage green (#6d7367): 3 parts ultramarine, 2 parts burnt sienna, 2 parts white, 1 part yellow
Brush: fan brush
Words:
1. First: Let the dark pines from step 4 dry, so this lighter green sits on top instead of mixing in.
2. Start: Start on the lowest sunny branches of the front pine, on the left side of the blue-green pines on the right.
3. As in step 4: Same fan brush and the same tapping, but a lighter green and a lighter touch, only on the branch edges facing the sun.
4. Strokes: Tap with the middle tips of the fan in short strokes slanting down and out along each branch, working up the tree.
5. Where the paint sits: Put it only on the branch edges that face the sun, in broken clumps up each tree. Most of it goes on the left side of the blue-green pines on the right.
6. Leave: Leave the dark from step 4 showing under and between every clump, and the shaded side of each tree mostly dark.
7. Stop when: Soft gray-green light catches the sunny branch edges in scattered clumps. If it hides the dark, you pressed too hard.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage builds the dark pines and the golden bushes that make the near woods.

## Step 6: Dab the dark of the golden bushes
Part of: The pines and the golden bushes
Picture: steps/06.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/06.jpg)
Paints:
- Deep ochre (#a4702c): 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow, a touch of white
Brush: old stiff brush
Words:
1. First: The pines and the fog must be dry.
2. Start: Start at the left edge of the canvas, where the top of the big bush mass is about 4 1/4 in from the top edge.
3. Strokes: Tap with the tip of a nearly dry brush, building a rounded mound of leaf dabs about 8 1/2 in wide.
4. Where the paint sits: Fill the mound most solidly low down, where the bushes are in shade. Then tap a second, low strip along the foot of the blue-green pines on the right, out to the right edge.
5. Leave: Leave small gaps of fog between dabs, and the gap between the two masses untouched: the path runs there.
6. Stop when: A big rounded mass of brownish-gold dabs at the left edge and a low strip by the pines. No lighter leaves yet. Blobs mean a wet brush: wipe it on a towel.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage builds the dark pines and the golden bushes that make the near woods.

## Step 7: Dab the middle gold on the bushes
Part of: The pines and the golden bushes
Picture: steps/07.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/07.jpg)
Paints:
- Golden yellow (#e78f1a): 2 parts yellow, 1 part red, a touch of ultramarine
Brush: old stiff brush
Words:
1. First: Let the dark dabs from step 6 dry, so the gold stays clean.
2. Start: Start along the top of the big bush mass at the left edge, about 4 1/4 in from the top edge.
3. As in step 6: The same tapping with the same stiff brush, now with Golden yellow over the dark dabs instead of covering bare fog.
4. Strokes: Tap with the tip, a few dabs at a time, working along the mound and then down it.
5. Where the paint sits: Cover most of the big mound, thickest where the light hits, plus a short row on the strip at the foot of the pines. Most of it goes on the left side of the golden undergrowth.
6. Leave: Leave the dark dabs from step 6 showing low down and between the gold dabs, so the bushes have depth.
7. Stop when: The big bush mass reads mostly warm gold, with dark showing beneath and between. The strip by the pines has just a few gold dabs.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage builds the dark pines and the golden bushes that make the near woods. By its end: Dark blue-green pines with soft gray-green light stand in the fog, and a big glowing gold bush mass faces them across a gap where the path will run, with fog still filling the ground.

## Step 9: Lay in the ground and the path
Part of: The ground and the rock
Picture: steps/09.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/09.jpg)
Paints:
- Rust orange (#dd771c): 3 parts yellow, 2 parts red, 2 parts burnt sienna
- Path tan (#c98947): 2 parts yellow, 2 parts red, 1 part ultramarine, 1 part white
Brush: 3/4 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the bushes dry, so the ground doesn't drag them.
2. Start: Start at the bottom edge with Rust orange and work up to about 7 in from the top edge, where the ground meets the bushes and pines.
3. Strokes: Keep the brush level and pull long strokes left and right across the ground, smoother toward the back and more textured in front.
4. Where the paint sits: Lay Path tan in a strip from the bottom edge near the middle, narrowing back to a point where the far woods begin. Blend its sides wet into the orange.
5. Leave: Leave the bushes, pines and fog untouched, except their very lowest edge, which the ground overlaps.
6. Stop when: The bottom of the canvas is warm orange ground from edge to edge, with a pale tan path narrowing back into the fog. No shadows, rock or loose leaves yet.

This stage, The ground and the rock: This stage lays the leafy ground with its pale path, a shadow, and the mossy rock.

## Step 10: Dry-brush the shadows on the ground
Part of: The ground and the rock
Picture: steps/10.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/10.jpg)
Paints:
- Brown shadow (#bf784b): 2 parts burnt sienna, 1 part white
Brush: 1 inch old stiff flat
Words:
1. First: The ground from step 9 must be dry before you dry-brush over it.
2. Start: Start on the near part of the path, where the patch's middle is about 8 3/4 in from the left edge.
3. As in step 3: The same dry brush and the same old stiff flat as the mist, now with a dark brown skimmed across the path.

## Step 11: Build the rock from pieces
Part of: The ground and the rock
Picture: steps/11.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/11.jpg)
Paints:
- Moss gray (#757159): 2 parts ultramarine, 1 part yellow, 1 part red, 1 part white
Brush: #8 round
Words:
1. First: Let the ground and bushes dry.
2. Start: Start with a wide, low oval whose left side is about 10 3/4 in from the left edge and whose top is about 7 in from the top edge, at the foot of the pines.
3. Strokes: Then add curved pieces on each side, like brackets, smaller toward the ends, to round it off.
4. Where the paint sits: Fill it flat and solid in one even color, with no shading yet. Keep its base flat where it sits on the ground.
5. Leave: Leave the bushes, pines and ground around it as they are.
6. Stop when: A flat olive-gray dome, about 2 3/4 in wide, sits on the ground at the foot of the pines, with no light or shadow yet.

This stage, The ground and the rock: This stage lays the leafy ground with its pale path, a shadow, and the mossy rock.

## Step 12: Shade the rock's right side
Part of: The ground and the rock

## Step 14: Pull the pale birch trunks
Part of: The birches

- Birch gray-white (#d9d1c6): 6 parts white, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow, a touch of ultramarine

## Step 22: Tap the sunlit leaves
Part of: The last lights
Picture: steps/22.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/22.jpg)
Paints:
- Sunlit lemon (#f9af47): 3 parts yellow, 3 parts white, 2 parts red
Brush: old stiff brush
Words:
1. First: Everything must be dry; these are the last touches.
2. Start: Start in the top left corner of the canvas, on the leaves overhead around the two big trunks, nearest the glow.
3. As in step 8: The same Sunlit lemon and tapping as the bush tops, now on the leaves overhead and along the path too.
