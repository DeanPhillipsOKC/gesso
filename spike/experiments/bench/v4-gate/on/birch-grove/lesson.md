# Lesson: birch grove

The step pictures are in steps/, and each step beside the one before it in pairs/.

## Before you start

You are painting a misty autumn birch grove, with a leaf-covered path running back into the fog. The two big leaning birches are the heart of it, pale bark against a glowing gold haze. Keep the far things soft and the near things bold, and the mist will do the rest.

The light: A low sun shines through the wood from behind the trees, so the gold bushes and leaves nearest it glow while the pines sit in cool blue shade. The birches and the rock catch that sun on the side facing it and fall into shadow away from it.

What this painting teaches:
- Dabbing (steps 6, 7, 8, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22): Dabbing is tapping with the tip of a dry brush, so the paint lands as separate dots that turn into leaves. It builds the bushes, the leaves overhead and the fallen leaves, always dark first, then middle, then the sunlit ones. Keep the brush dry and lightly loaded, or the dots turn into blobs.
- Dry brush (steps 3, 10, 15, 17): Dry brush is a nearly dry brush skimmed lightly so the paint catches only the canvas texture. It makes the mist, the shadows on the path, and the soft shade and light on the birch bark. If it goes on solid, the brush was too loaded.
- Thick to thin (steps 2, 14, 16): Thick to thin is pressing the brush's full width, then easing onto its edge so the mark tapers. It pulls the far trunks and the birches, wide at the foot and narrow as they rise, and makes the short dark bark marks.

The way there: First comes the misty background, the farthest thing, so the depth is there before anything solid. Then the near woods: the pines and the golden bushes. Next the ground with its path and the mossy rock. The birches, the subject, go on over all of it, and then the gold leaves overhead and the fallen leaves go over the trunks. The brightest sunlit leaves come last. The first layers stop where the ground will cover them, so bare canvas showing early on is expected.

This stage, The misty background: This stage lays the soft, misty fog that everything else stands in.

## Step 1: Blend the misty glow
Part of: The misty background
Picture: steps/01.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/01.jpg)
Paints:
- Birch gray-white (#d9d1c6): 6 parts white, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow, a touch of ultramarine
- Golden yellow (#e78f1a): 2 parts yellow, 1 part red, a touch of ultramarine
- Haze blue (#9bb2ce): 2 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. Start: Start at the top edge near the left edge with Birch gray-white and a little Golden yellow, and work toward the right edge.
2. Strokes: Make short criss-cross X strokes, picking up Haze blue as you move right, overlapping so the colors melt together.
3. Where the paint sits: Most of the glow ends up soft Haze blue. Keep a faint warm streak of Golden yellow and Birch gray-white low on the left side of the glow, where the low sun shines through.
4. Leave: The layer stops short at a loose, uneven lower edge about 6 1/4 in from the top edge, not a ruled line. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 4.
5. Stop when: Stop when the top of the canvas is a soft, cool fog with a faint warm glow, and the criss-cross marks still show.

This stage, The misty background: This stage lays the soft, misty fog that everything else stands in.

## Step 2: Pull the far misty trunks
Part of: The misty background
Picture: steps/02.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/02.jpg)
Paints:
- Misty blue-gray (#a1afc0): 3 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow, a touch of burnt sienna
Brush: 1/2 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the glow dry first, so the trunks stay crisp.
2. Start: Start with one thin trunk whose left side is about 3 1/2 in from the left edge, beginning low in the glow.
3. Strokes: Press the brush's full width at the trunk's foot, then turn onto the edge and lighten as you pull up toward the top edge, so it tapers thin.
4. Where the paint sits: That trunk runs almost the whole height of the glow. Add a short, soft dab of trunk about 2 in from the left edge, and one short piece about 11 in from the left edge.
5. Leave: Leave the soft fog showing all round these few marks. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 4.
6. Stop when: Stop with one clear thin trunk and two small soft marks, faint and far away in the fog, with no bark marks.

This stage, The misty background: This stage lays the soft, misty fog that everything else stands in. By its end: A soft blue fog with a faint warm glow and a few far trunks fills the back of the scene; the rest of the canvas is still bare.

## Step 3: Dry-brush the mist down the path
Part of: The misty background
Picture: steps/03.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/03.jpg)
Paints:
- Mist white (#d6e0ee): 12 parts white, 1 part ultramarine
Brush: 1 inch old stiff flat
Words:
1. First: The far trunks must be dry before you skim over them.
2. Start: Start low in the middle of the misty glow, where the bottoms of the far trunks fade into the fog.
3. Strokes: With a nearly dry brush, skim lightly side to side, barely touching the canvas, so the paint catches only the texture.
4. Where the paint sits: Keep it to the lowest part of the glow in the middle, over the feet of the far trunks, where the path will later vanish into fog.
5. Leave: Leave the trunks' upper parts and the rest of the glow untouched. The bare canvas stays bare until step 4.
6. Stop when: Stop while it is barely visible: a faint veil that softens the trunk bottoms. If you can see a clear patch of paint, it is too much.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage adds the dark pines and the glowing golden bushes in front of the fog.

## Step 4: Stamp the dark pines
Part of: The pines and the golden bushes
Picture: steps/04.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/04.jpg)
Paints:
- Pine blue-green (#586e80): 1 part ultramarine, 1 part white, a touch of burnt sienna
Brush: fan brush
Words:
1. First: Let the glow dry first.
2. Start: Start at the tip of the tallest pine, near the top edge, about 13 in from the left edge, then do a shorter pine each side.
3. Strokes: Stamp with the middle tips of the fan, working down each pine in a zigzag. Press harder as you go down, so each pine widens toward the ground.
4. Where the paint sits: The pines run from about 9 3/4 in from the left edge to the right edge. Leave small gaps where the fog shows through.
5. Leave: The layer stops short at a loose, uneven lower edge about 8 3/4 in from the top edge, going onto bare canvas there. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 9.
6. Stop when: Stop when three soft, dark, ragged pines stand over the far trunks, with small fog gaps and no light on them.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage adds the dark pines and the glowing golden bushes in front of the fog.

## Step 5: Tap light on the pines' left side
Part of: The pines and the golden bushes
Picture: steps/05.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/05.jpg)

- Sage green (#6d7367): 3 parts ultramarine, 2 parts burnt sienna, 2 parts white, 1 part yellow
Brush: fan brush
Words:
1. First: The dark pines must be dry first.
2. Start: Start on the left edge of the tallest pine's upper branches, about 13 in from the left edge, where the sun from the upper left reaches.
3. As in step 4: Same fan tapping as the dark pines, but lighter, and only on the sunlit branch edges and the small fog gaps.

5. Where the paint sits: Put the light on the sun-facing left edges of the branches on each of the three pines, and into the small fog gaps, which it fills.
6. Leave: Leave the dark showing between the light taps and on the shaded right parts of each pine. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 9.
7. Stop when: Stop when the pines have soft gray-green light along their left sides with dark showing beneath. They should still read as mostly dark.

This stage, The pines and the golden bushes: This stage adds the dark pines and the glowing golden bushes in front of the fog.

## Step 9: Lay in the ground and the path
Part of: The ground and the rock
Picture: steps/09.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/09.jpg)
Paints:
- Rust orange (#dd771c): 3 parts yellow, 2 parts red, 2 parts burnt sienna
- Path tan (#c98947): 2 parts yellow, 2 parts red, 1 part ultramarine, 1 part white
Brush: 3/4 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the bushes dry first.
2. Start: Start at the bottom edge of the canvas and work up to the loose lower edge of the bushes and pines, about 7 in from the top edge.
3. Strokes: Keep the brush level and pull long strokes left and right across the canvas, row after row, with Rust orange.
4. Where the paint sits: Lay a strip of Path tan from the bottom edge near the middle of the canvas, narrowing as it goes back to the fog. Make the front darker and rougher, the back smoother.
5. Leave: Cover all the bare canvas, and slightly overlap the lower edge of the bushes and pines. Leave the fog showing where the path meets it.
6. Stop when: Stop when the whole lower canvas is warm orange ground with a pale path running back into the fog. No shadows or rock yet.

This stage, The ground and the rock: This stage covers the ground with warm leaf color, runs the path back into the fog, and sets the mossy rock on it.
