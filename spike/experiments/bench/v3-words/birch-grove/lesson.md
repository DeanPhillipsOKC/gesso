# Lesson: birch grove

The step pictures are in steps/, and each step beside the one before it in pairs/.

## Step 1: Blend the misty sky over the whole canvas
Part of: The misty sky
Picture: steps/01.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/01.jpg)
Paints:
- Pale gold (#d2c8b8): 4 parts white, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow, a touch of ultramarine
- Misty blue (#90abd5): 8 parts white, 3 parts ultramarine
- Blue-violet mist (#7196d8): 3 parts white, 2 parts ultramarine, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1 1/2 inch flat
Words:
1. Inside the outlined area. It reaches all four edges of the canvas.
2. Paints: Pale gold for the glow, Misty blue across the rest, and Blue-violet mist for the soft upright streaks, as the after picture shows.
3. Mix enough Pale gold for this step and steps 11 and 20; cover it between them.
4. Mix enough Blue-violet mist for this step and steps 3 and 6; cover it between them.
5. Mix enough Misty blue for this step and step 4; cover it between them.
6. Brush: 1 1/2 inch flat. Load: Creamy paint. An even coat.
7. Start with Pale gold at the glow. Without rinsing, work Misty blue out from it, stroking back over the join. Then drag in upright Blue-violet mist streaks.
8. It should leave a soft, even haze, with gentle joins where two wet colors meet.
9. Watch out: Blending after the paint has started to drag. Acrylic sets in minutes, and scrubbing setting paint lifts it and leaves streaks. Fix: stop as soon as it drags.
10. Let it dry, then blend a new thin layer on top.
Tip: The light comes from the low sun, shining out of the Pale gold glow, so keep that glow where the after picture has it and let Misty blue fill the rest. Blend mostly side to side, but the after picture already shows faint upright streaks of Blue-violet mist, like ghost trunks in the fog: drag a few down softly and let them stay blurred.

## Step 2: Circle in the glowing sun
Part of: The misty sky
Picture: steps/02.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/02.jpg)
Paints:
- Sun cream (#fbdb94): 6 parts white, 2 parts yellow, 1 part red
Brush: #8 round
Words:
1. Let the misty sky dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Rinse the brush fully first: this is the lightest paint yet.
3. Inside the outlined area. Its top is about 1 1/4 in from the top edge, and its left side is at the left edge; it's about 1 1/4 in wide and 2 in tall.
4. The outline also runs under where the birches' shading and bark patches, and the autumn leaves will go: paint straight through there.
5. The outline also reaches onto the misty sky, painted already: there, only soften this paint into its edge and leave it showing.
6. Paint: Sun cream.
7. Brush: #8 round.
8. Small circles with the tip of the brush make a bumpy edge. Around a bright spot, circular strokes move outward from it.
9. It should leave a bumpy, soft edge.
10. Watch out: The edge is too hard. Fix: soften it with lighter passes. Smudging with a finger works too.
11. Step back, squint, or take a photo to see it whole, and compare the big shapes with the picture.
Tip: The sun is taller than it is wide: about 1 1/4 in wide and 2 1/4 in tall, at the left edge of the canvas, with its top about 1 1/4 in from the top edge. Start at its middle, about 2 1/4 in from the top edge, and circle outward, letting a few short strokes fan out from it like the rays in the after picture.

## Step 3: Pull the misty far trunks
Part of: The far woods
Picture: steps/03.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/03.jpg)
Paints:
- Blue-violet mist (#7196d8): 3 parts white, 2 parts ultramarine, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1/4 inch flat
Words:
1. Let the misty sky dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Rinse the brush well first. Dirty water tints every color with the last one.
3. Inside the outlined area. Its top is about 3 1/2 in from the top edge, and its left side is about 6 1/2 in from the left edge; it's about 1 1/2 in wide and 2 1/2 in tall.
4. The outline also runs under where the autumn leaves and the front birch trunks will go: paint straight through there.
5. The outline also reaches onto the misty sky, painted already: there, only soften this paint into its edge and leave it showing.
6. Paint: Blue-violet mist.
7. Brush: 1/4 inch flat.
8. Press the full width of the brush at the bottom of the trunk, then turn onto its edge and lighten the pressure as you pull up.
9. It should leave a thin trunk that starts wide at the bottom and tapers to a point at the top.
10. Watch out: Trunks that look like stiff sticks, every one the same width. Fix: start thick at the bottom and taper, and let the trunks differ in width and lean a little.
Tip: These trunks sit far back in the mist, so keep them thin, pale and soft-edged, with no light side yet. Pull each one straight up and down where the Where picture shows; the misty far trunks are about 1 1/2 in wide and 2 3/4 in tall, their top about 3 1/2 in from the top edge. The after picture changes only a little, so leave the misty sky showing between the trunks.

## Step 4: Stamp the fir trees
Part of: The far woods
Picture: steps/04.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/04.jpg)
Paints:
- Deep pine green (#364f68): 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow, a touch of burnt sienna, a touch of white
- Misty blue (#90abd5): 8 parts white, 3 parts ultramarine
Brush: fan brush
Words:
1. Let the misty sky dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its top is about 3/4 in from the top edge, and its right side is at the right edge; it's about 6 1/2 in wide and 6 1/2 in tall.
3. The outline also runs under where the autumn leaves, and the birches' shading and bark patches will go: paint straight through there.
4. The outline also reaches onto the misty sky and the misty far trunks, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
5. Paints, darkest first: Deep pine green, then Misty blue. The paint map shows where each goes.
6. Mix enough Deep pine green for this step and step 13; cover it between them.
7. Brush: fan brush. Load: Thin paint. Stroke the brush on the palette until the bristles fan out.
8. Stamp only the tips, using the middle of the fan, not the side bristles. For a shape that widens, work from the top down in a zigzag and press harder as you go down.
9. Short strokes in different directions make a rough texture.
10. It should leave clusters of fine, needle-like marks, light at the top and heavier where pressed.
11. Watch out: A heavy, solid top. Too much pressure or paint at the start of the tree. Fix: add fine branches with a small round instead of more fan marks.
Tip: Stamp the dark firs in Deep pine green first, then the paler hazy firs in Misty blue on the left side of the fir trees, where the after picture has them. Keep each fir narrow at its top and wider toward its bottom, letting the bottoms fade into the mist; most of the paint is on the right side of the fir trees, which reaches the right edge of the canvas.

## Step 9: Dab the dark leaves everywhere
Part of: The autumn leaves
Picture: steps/09.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/09.jpg)
Paints:
- Burnt orange (#bb671c): 2 parts burnt sienna, 1 part yellow, 1 part red, a touch of white
- Moss green (#6f635c): 3 parts burnt sienna, 2 parts ultramarine, 2 parts white, a touch of yellow
- Leaf brown (#877e7a): 1 part ultramarine, 1 part burnt sienna, 1 part white, a touch of yellow
Brush: old stiff brush
Words:
1. Let the forest floor and the far woods dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. It reaches all four edges of the canvas.
3. Paints, darkest first: Moss green, Burnt orange, then Leaf brown. The paint map shows where each goes.
4. Mix enough Moss green for this step and steps 12 and 16; cover it between them.
5. Brush: old stiff brush. Load: A light load. No water on the brush: a wet one makes blobs.
6. Tap with the tip of the brush. The more you tap one spot, the more solid it gets.
7. It should leave dots or dappled patches that get more solid the more you tap.
8. Watch out: Blobs instead of dots. The brush is too wet or holds too much paint. Fix: wipe most of the paint off on a paper towel and tap again.
Tip: Most of what this step adds is in the bottom half of the canvas: the leaf floor and the bushes beside the path, with a few dabs hanging in the treetops near the top edge, as the after picture has them. Tap the Moss green bush first, where the after picture has it, then the Burnt orange, then scatter Leaf brown over the ground and the path. Leave small gaps where the forest floor peeks through between the dabs, as the after picture shows.

## Step 13: Shade the rock's underside
Part of: The mossy rock

## Step 18: Line the thin bark marks
Part of: The birch trunks
Picture: steps/18.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/18.jpg)
Paints:
- Bark dark (#584b4a): 3 parts burnt sienna, 2 parts ultramarine, 1 part white
Brush: #2 liner
Words:
1. Let the birches' shading and bark patches, and the front birch trunks dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its bottom is about 1 1/4 in from the bottom edge, and its right side is at the right edge; it's about 13 1/2 in wide and 7 1/2 in tall.
3. The outline also runs under where the sunlight on the birches and the sunlit leaves on the ground will go: paint straight through there.
4. The outline also reaches onto the birches' shading and bark patches, and the front birch trunks, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
5. Paint: Bark dark.
6. Brush: #2 liner. Load: Thin paint. Swirl the brush in water, then in the paint. Add water if the line skips. Reload often.
7. Hold the brush upright, with light, steady pressure. Pull each line across the trunk, starting thick and lightening as you go, and wobble the edges a little.
8. It should leave thin, flowing lines.
9. Watch out: Skippy, broken lines. The paint is too thick. Fix: add water.
10. Step back, squint, or take a photo to see it whole, and compare the big shapes with the picture.
Tip: Pull short, thin lines across each trunk between the Bark dark patches, wobbling as they go; most of it is on the left side and in the top half. Keep them few and fine: the after picture looks almost the same as the before, so stop while the trunks still read pale. Leave the left edges of the trunks as they are; their sunlight comes in step 19.

## Step 19: Catch the sunlight on the birches
Part of: The last lights
Picture: steps/19.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/19.jpg)
Paints:
- Birch white (#aca2a8): 8 parts white, 3 parts burnt sienna, 2 parts ultramarine, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1/2 inch flat
Words:
1. Let the birch trunks and the autumn leaves dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its top is at the top edge, and its right side is at the right edge; it's about 14 1/2 in wide and 8 in tall.
3. The outline also runs under where the sunlit leaves on the ground will go: paint straight through there.
4. The outline also reaches onto the birches' shading and bark patches, and the autumn leaves, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
5. Paint: Birch white.
6. Brush: 1/2 inch flat. Load: Wiped nearly dry. Only the tip goes in the paint, and no water on the brush. It should feel dry and hard to apply.
7. Light pressure, skimming the surface.
8. It should leave feathery, broken, see-through marks that catch the canvas texture. Never a solid area.
9. Watch out: It went on solid. The brush was too loaded. Fix: let it dry, then dry-brush the base color back over part of it.
Tip: The light comes from the upper left, so skim the Birch white down the left side of each birch only, pulling from the top down, and leave the right side of each trunk in shade. Most of it goes on the two big birches in the left half of the canvas, as the after picture shows. Skip over the dark bark patches and let them show through.

## Step 20: Tap the sunlit leaves on the ground
Part of: The last lights
Picture: steps/20.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/20.jpg)
Paints:
- Pale gold (#d2c8b8): 4 parts white, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of yellow, a touch of ultramarine
Brush: #12 round
Words:
1. Let the sunlight on the birches and the birch trunks dry to the touch first. A hair dryer on low speeds it up.
2. Inside the outlined area. Its bottom is about 1/2 in from the bottom edge, and its left side is at the left edge; it's about 15 1/2 in wide and 3 1/2 in tall.
3. The outline also reaches onto the autumn leaves and the forest floor, painted already: there, only soften this paint into their edge and leave them showing.
4. Paint: Pale gold.
5. Brush: #12 round. Load: A light load. No water on the brush: a wet one makes blobs.
6. Tap with the tip of the brush. The more you tap one spot, the more solid it gets.
7. It should leave dots or dappled patches that get more solid the more you tap.
8. Pale gold is much lighter than what it goes over: expect two or three thin coats, letting each dry.
9. Watch out: Blobs instead of dots. The brush is too wet or holds too much paint. Fix: wipe most of the paint off on a paper towel and tap again.
10. Step back, squint, or take a photo to see it whole, and compare the big shapes with the picture.
Tip: Most of the Pale gold goes in the right half, thickest on the path, where the after picture has it, with only a few scattered leaves near the left edge. Tap separate leaf-sized dabs, not a solid band, and leave the darker leaves showing between them so the ground still reads as a pile of leaves.
