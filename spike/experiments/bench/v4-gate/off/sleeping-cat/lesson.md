# Lesson: sleeping cat

The step pictures are in steps/, and each step beside the one before it in pairs/.

## Before you start

You're about to paint a gray tabby cat fast asleep on a chunky yellow blanket, in a sunny corner by a window. The heart of it is the sleeping face: closed eyes, a pink nose and a white muzzle by the paws. Warm sun and cozy colors make it feel calm and soft.

The light: The window light falls into the room across the sill and onto the cat and blanket. Where it lands, the wood glows peach, the blanket's stitches turn pale, and the cat's fur looks lighter; the far end of the cat stays in shadow.

What this painting teaches:
- Building from pieces (steps 5, 6, 7, 10, 13): You build big shapes from simple ones: a rectangle for the frame, an oval for the cat, then curved pieces added round it. It turns a hard shape into a few easy ones. Soften where pieces meet only when the thing should look smooth.
- Press and lift (steps 11, 12, 17, 20): Touch down with the tip, press so the brush spreads, then pull and lift to a point, making a teardrop. Here it makes the blanket's knit stitches, the pink inside the ears and the tiny nose. If you get blobs, use less paint and lift faster.
- Dry brush (steps 8, 9): Only the tip of a dry brush touches the paint, and you skim lightly so the marks break up over the canvas. It gives the sill its streaky wood and its soft patch of sun. If it goes on solid, the brush held too much paint.

The way there: First comes the view through the window, a patch of pale sky and soft leaves, because it is the farthest thing. Next the blue wall, then the white window frame with its lavender shadows. The wooden sill goes in after that, darkened and then lit by the sun. The knitted blanket follows as a golden roll with dark and light stitches. Last comes the cat, painted over everything: its shape and shading, white patches, stripes, ears and fur, and finally the sleeping face.

This stage, The view through the window: This stage paints the far view: a soft patch of sky with a small clump of leaves.

## Step 1: Blend the pale sky in the window
Part of: The view through the window
Picture: steps/01.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/01.jpg)
Paints:
- Pale sky blue (#98cdf7): 3 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of yellow
- Warm white (#fef5e1): 12 parts white, 1 part yellow, a touch of red
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. Start: Start in the upper left corner of the canvas with Pale sky blue. This patch of sky reaches the left edge and the top edge.
2. Strokes: Stroke side to side, working down. Without rinsing, pick up Warm white and paint the lower part of the patch, then stroke back up over the join several times while both are wet.
3. Where the paint sits: Keep the strongest blue at the top and let it fade to near white lower down.
4. Leave: Let the patch end in a soft, ragged edge; its middle is about 1 1/4 in from the left edge. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 4.
5. Stop when: Stop when the corner holds a small, soft patch of sky, blue on top and paler below, with no hard join.

This stage, The view through the window: This stage paints the far view: a soft patch of sky with a small clump of leaves.

## Step 2: Dab the green leaves outside
Part of: The view through the window
Picture: steps/02.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/02.jpg)
Paints:
- Leaf green (#89af9d): 3 parts white, 2 parts ultramarine, 1 part yellow, a touch of burnt sienna
Brush: old stiff flat brush
Words:
1. First: Let the sky from step 1 dry first, so the green dots stay crisp.
2. Start: Start low in the sky patch, near the left edge, about 2 in from the top edge.
3. Strokes: Wipe most of the paint off the dry brush, then tap with its tip. Tap a few times in one spot to make a small cluster of leaves.
4. Where the paint sits: Keep the leaves to one small clump low in the sky patch. These are the shaded leaves, so they sit under where the sunny ones will go.
5. Leave: Leave pale sky showing all around the clump and between the dots. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 4.
6. Stop when: Stop when there is one soft, dotty clump of green, smaller than a thumbprint, with sky peeking through.

This stage, The view through the window: This stage paints the far view: a soft patch of sky with a small clump of leaves. By its end: A small patch of pale blue sky holds a soft green clump of leaves, and the rest of the canvas is still bare.

## Step 3: Tap the sunny leaves
Part of: The view through the window
Picture: steps/03.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/03.jpg)
Paints:
- Light leaf green (#b5c89c): 3 parts white, 1 part yellow, 1 part ultramarine, a touch of red
Brush: old stiff flat brush
Words:
1. First: Let the dark green dabs from step 2 dry first.
2. Start: Start on the top of the green clump from step 2, the side that faces the light.
3. As in step 2: the same dry tapping, but with a sunnier green, and only where the light hits the clump, so the darker taps still show.
4. Strokes: Use the same dry, wiped brush and tap lightly with its tip, a few taps at a time.
5. Where the paint sits: Put the sunny taps on the tops of the clump, plus one or two small loose taps near the left edge, where the sun catches stray leaves.
6. Leave: Leave the darker green taps showing underneath and the pale sky around the clump. The rest of the canvas stays bare until step 4.
7. Stop when: Stop when the clump looks lit on top and darker below, still small and soft, like a far tree seen through a window.

This stage, The blue wall: This stage fills the room's blue walls with loose criss-cross strokes. By its end: Two blue walls meet at a deeper blue corner, with the window view showing, and the rest of the canvas is still bare.

## Step 4: Criss-cross the blue wall
Part of: The blue wall
Picture: steps/04.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/04.jpg)
Paints:
- Wall blue (#4f90dc): 1 part ultramarine, 1 part white, a touch of yellow
- Light wall blue (#68a0e0): 3 parts white, 2 parts ultramarine, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the leaves from step 3 dry first.
2. Start: Start at the top edge, a little right of the window sky, with Wall blue and Light wall blue on the brush.
3. Strokes: Cross short strokes in two directions, like overlapping X shapes, working across and down, and let the two blues mix on the canvas.
4. Where the paint sits: The blue wall reaches the left, right and top edges. Press a deeper band of Wall blue down the corner where the walls meet, and paint around the sky and leaves.
5. Leave: Leave the window sky and leaves showing. End the blue at a slanted line where the wall meets the floor, with a loose edge.
6. Stop when: Stop when the wall is loose criss-cross blue with lighter patches. The bottom of the canvas stays bare until step 7.

This stage, The window frame: This stage builds the white window frame and gives it depth with lavender shadows.

## Step 5: Block in the white frame
Part of: The window frame
Picture: steps/05.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/05.jpg)
Paints:
- Warm white (#fef5e1): 12 parts white, 1 part yellow, a touch of red
Brush: 1/2 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the wall from step 4 dry first, so the white stays clean.
2. Start: Start with the tall post of the window frame, a long rectangle right of the window sky, running down from the top edge.
3. Strokes: Lay the post as one rectangle, then add the crossbar across the window and the deep lower ledge as smaller pieces joined to it.
4. Where the paint sits: The white window frame sits over the edge of the sky and trees and the blue around them. The ledge slants down toward the post.
5. Leave: Leave sky and leaves showing in the two panes the crossbar divides, and the deeper blue corner band of the wall.
6. Stop when: Stop when there is a flat creamy frame: a post, a crossbar and a ledge, about 3 1/4 in wide, with no shading yet.

This stage, The window frame: This stage builds the white window frame and gives it depth with lavender shadows. By its end: A creamy white window frame with lavender shadow strips stands against the blue walls, and the rest of the canvas is still bare.

## Step 6: Shade the frame lavender
Part of: The window frame
Picture: steps/06.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/06.jpg)
Paints:
- Lavender gray (#a299b5): 3 parts white, 1 part ultramarine, 1 part burnt sienna
Brush: 1/4 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the white frame from step 5 dry first.
2. Start: Start on the inner face of the post, the narrow side that faces into the window.
3. As in step 5: you build the frame in pieces again, but now as thin strips with a smaller brush, only on the faces turned away from the light.
4. Strokes: Lay each shadow as a thin strip with the flat edge of the brush, piece by piece, along the length of each part of the frame.
5. Where the paint sits: Put the lavender on the faces that turn away from the window light: the inner face of the post, the underside of the crossbar, and along the top of the ledge.
6. Leave: Leave the fronts of the post, crossbar and ledge creamy white, so the frame looks lit from outside.
7. Stop when: Stop when the frame looks deep and solid, with soft lavender strips on its inner faces and bright white fronts.

This stage, The wooden sill: This stage lays the wooden sill and then puts window sunlight across it.

## Step 7: Block in the wooden sill
Part of: The wooden sill
Picture: steps/07.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/07.jpg)
Paints:
- Wood brown (#a7421d): 1 part red, 1 part burnt sienna, a touch of ultramarine, a touch of white
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the blue wall dry first, so its lower edge stays clean.
2. Start: Start where the wall meets the floor, at the foot of the deeper blue corner band, and work out along that slanted line.
3. As in step 5: you build from flat pieces again, but much bigger, with the wide brush, to fill the whole floor area.
4. Strokes: Lay the sill as broad flat pieces with the brush, side to side, filling down to the bottom edge.
5. Where the paint sits: The wooden windowsill fills the whole bottom of the canvas, from the left edge to the right edge. Its top is about 7 1/2 in from the top edge, under the corner.
6. Leave: Leave the blue wall, the white window frame and its ledge showing; only the floor area gets brown.
7. Stop when: Stop when everything below the wall is a flat, even rust-brown, with no shading or sun yet.

This stage, The wooden sill: This stage lays the wooden sill and then puts window sunlight across it.

## Step 8: Darken the sill's front edge
Part of: The wooden sill
Picture: steps/08.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/08.jpg)
Paints:
- Dark wood brown (#8b432c): 3 parts red, 2 parts ultramarine, 2 parts burnt sienna, 1 part white
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the rust-brown from step 7 dry first.
2. Start: Start at the bottom edge, at the front of the sill, and work up and across.
3. Strokes: Dip only the tip in the paint, wipe it, and skim side to side with light pressure. Build it up in several light passes, never a solid coat.
4. Where the paint sits: Darken most of the wooden windowsill: the front strip near the bottom edge, the far part under the wall, and the right end.
5. Leave: Leave one wide slanted patch of the plain rust-brown a little in from the left edge, where the sun will fall, plus a thin slanted bar of it nearer the edge.
6. Stop when: Stop when the sill looks darker and streaky like wood, with one slanted patch still plain. If it went solid, let it dry and skim rust-brown back over.

This stage, The wooden sill: This stage lays the wooden sill and then puts window sunlight across it. By its end: The rust-brown sill is darkened and streaky like wood, with a warm slanted patch of sunlight glowing on it.

## Step 9: Lay the sun patches on the sill
Part of: The wooden sill
Picture: steps/09.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/09.jpg)
Paints:
- Sunlit peach (#dd7447): 1 part red, 1 part burnt sienna, 1 part white, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1 inch flat
Words:
1. First: Let the dark streaks from step 8 dry first.
2. Start: Start in the slanted patch of plain rust-brown that you left in step 8, at its end nearest the window.

6. Leave: Leave the thin dark bar between the two sunny patches: it is the shadow of the window frame. Leave the dark streaks elsewhere.
7. Stop when: Stop when there is a warm glowing slanted patch on the sill, brightest near the window, streaky at its edges.

This stage, The knitted blanket: This stage lays the blanket as one golden roll, then knits it with dark and light stitches.

## Step 11: Press in the dark stitches
Part of: The knitted blanket
Picture: steps/11.jpg (beside the step before: pairs/11.jpg)
Paints:
- Dark ochre (#d57903): 2 parts yellow, 1 part red, 1 part burnt sienna
Brush: #8 round
Words:
1. First: Let the golden roll from step 10 dry first.
2. Start: Start along the bottom of the roll, near its rounded left end.
3. Strokes: Touch down with the tip, press, then pull and lift, so each stitch is a small teardrop. Pair them into V shapes in rows that follow the roll's curve.
4. Where the paint sits: Put the dark stitches all over the blanket, thickest along its bottom and in the long fold where the top layer turns over at the left end.
5. Leave: Leave the flat yellow from step 10 showing between every stitch, so they read as separate loops.
6. Stop when: Stop when the blanket looks like a folded chunky knit, with rows of dark V stitches curving along it. If you make blobs, use less paint and lift faster.

This stage, The knitted blanket: This stage lays the blanket as one golden roll, then knits it with dark and light stitches. By its end: A chunky knitted blanket lies folded across the sill, shadowed in its fold and lit where the sun falls.

## Step 12: Press in the light stitches
Part of: The knitted blanket

## Step 16: Pull the dark tabby stripes
Part of: The stripes and fur

- Charcoal (#493f40): 3 parts ultramarine, 3 parts burnt sienna, 1 part white, a touch of yellow
Brush: 1/4 inch angle
Words:

## Step 21: Pull the white whiskers
